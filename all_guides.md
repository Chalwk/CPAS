## Overview

Arthur's Pass is the most well-known VFR crossing of the Southern Alps. It links [Christchurch (NZCH)](/guides/nzch/) on the east coast with [Hokitika (NZHK)](/guides/nzhk/) on the west, following the Waimakariri River valley to the pass and descending to the coast.

{% include region_map.html %}

## Route

1. Depart [Christchurch (NZCH)](/guides/nzch/). Expect a clearance westbound via the Waimakariri River corridor.
2. Follow the **Waimakariri River** upstream through the gorge.
3. Cross **Arthur's Pass** (3,018 ft pass saddle) at a minimum of **7,500 ft MSL** - higher if terrain clearance or turbulence demands it.
4. Descend west of the divide into the **Otira Valley** and then the **Taramakau River**.
5. Continue west, descending toward the coast and joining the Hokitika aerodrome area (CTAF 119.1).

## Reporting Points

- **Waimakariri Gorge** - about 25 NM north-west of Christchurch, entry to the corridor.
- **Arthur's Pass** - the saddle itself.
- **Otira** - western side of the divide.
- **Kumara** - coastal junction.

## Minimum Altitudes

- **7,500 ft MSL** over the pass and the main divide.
- **2,000 ft AGL** minimum over national park land.

## Frequencies

| Service                                  | Frequency |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------- |
| Christchurch Approach / Departure        | 120.9     |
| Christchurch Information (Hokitika area) | 118.5     |
| Hokitika CTAF                            | 119.1     |
| Area / Mountain radio                    | 130.6     |

## Hazards

- **Mountain wave** and downdrafts on the lee side.
- Rapid cloud build-up - the pass can close in minutes.
- The Waimakariri valley has multiple braided channels - easy to mistake your position.
- Nor'west arch gives way to cloud and rain west of the divide.

## Tips

- Cross early in the day; cloud builds by afternoon.
- File a flight plan - there are limited landing options on the divide.
- If the pass is closed, consider [Haast Pass](/guides/haast-pass/) as an alternative.

## Overview

Banks Peninsula is a volcanic landmass immediately south-east of Christchurch. Its two harbours - Lyttelton and Akaroa - are the dominant features, and the peninsula is a popular scenic and training area.

{% include region_map.html %}

## Route

1. Depart [Christchurch (NZCH)](/guides/nzch/) and track south of the city.
2. Cross the **Port Hills** at a safe altitude (2,500 ft MSL minimum recommended).
3. Overfly **Lyttelton Harbour** and **Quail Island**.
4. Continue east/south-east over the peninsula's rugged interior.
5. Overfly **Akaroa Harbour** and **Akaroa township**.
6. Return to Christchurch via the northern coast or back over the Port Hills.

## Reporting Points

- **Christchurch CTR boundary** - on entry/exit.
- **Sign of the Takahe / Summit Road** - Port Hills crossing.
- **Lyttelton** - harbour.
- **Akaroa** - eastern harbour.
- **Godley Head** - northern tip.

## Minimum Altitudes

- **4,000 ft MSL** over the peninsula interior (terrain reaches about 3,000 ft near Mt Herbert); 2,500 ft MSL recommended over the Port Hills.
- **500 ft AGL** minimum over water and unpopulated areas.
- Remain **clear of Christchurch Class C airspace** - no shortcuts through the CTR without clearance.

## Frequencies

| Service                  | Frequency |
| ------------------------ | --------- |
| Christchurch Tower       | 118.4     |
| Christchurch Approach    | 120.9     |
| Christchurch Information | 129.3     |
| Banks Peninsula CTAF     | 123.1     |

## Hazards

- **Nor'wester** produces strong turbulence and lee downdrafts on the Port Hills.
- High recreational traffic.
- Terrain is genuinely rugged - don't descend over the interior.
- Two harbours mean plenty of boat masts near the coast.

## Tips

- Use the Summit Road as a visual line over the Port Hills.
- Akaroa is a popular lunch stop - the airstrip is short and sloped.
- Watch for Christchurch arrival/departure traffic when re-entering the CTR.

## Overview

The Franz Josef and Fox glaciers descend from the Southern Alps almost to sea level on the West Coast. Their valleys are the most popular scenic corridors in the country and are flown by helicopter and fixed-wing operations daily.

{% include region_map.html %}

## Route

1. Depart [Hokitika (NZHK)](/guides/nzhk/) or the glacier fields directly.
2. Track south along the coast past **Whataroa**.
3. Turn inland into the **Waiho Valley** for Franz Josef, or the **Fox Valley** for Fox Glacier.
4. Climb into the glacier valley to view the icefall.
5. Optional glacier landing (helicopters only) - check conditions.

## Reporting Points

- **Hokitika** - coastal departure.
- **Whataroa** - coastal township, continue south toward the glacier valleys.
- **Franz Josef township** - tourist hub, helicopter base.
- **Fox Glacier township** - southern glacier base.
- **Glacier terminal faces** - the icefall viewpoints.

## Minimum Altitudes

- **2,000 ft AGL** minimum over national park land.
- **500 ft AGL** over the coast only where permitted.
- For glacier landings: check current snow/ice conditions and DO NOT land without proper training.

## Frequencies

| Service                | Frequency |
| ---------------------- | --------- |
| Hokitika CTAF          | 119.1     |
| West Coast common CTAF | 123.1     |
| Area / Mountain radio  | 130.6     |

## Hazards

- **Katabatic winds** - cold air flows down the glaciers and can produce sudden downdrafts.
- Glacier surfaces are unstable - never land on a glacier without special training.
- Heavy helicopter traffic in summer.
- Rapid cloud build-up - the valleys can close in minutes.

## Tips

- Do not commit to a valley unless you have a clear escape route back out.
- Monitor 123.1 for other operators' position reports.
- If weather is marginal, stay coastal and view the glaciers from the seaward end.

## Overview

Haast Pass (563 m / 1,847 ft) is the lowest of the three main alpine crossings (with Arthur's and Lewis). It links the West Coast at Haast with the upper Clutha valley at [Wanaka (NZWF)](/guides/nzwf/) and [Queenstown (NZQN)](/guides/nzqn/).

{% include region_map.html %}

## Route

1. Depart from [Hokitika (NZHK)](/guides/nzhk/) or Franz Josef / Fox for the West Coast leg.
2. Track south along the coast to **Haast**.
3. Turn east into the **Haast River** valley.
4. Follow the valley through **Haast Pass**, crossing at a minimum of **6,500 ft MSL**.
5. Descend into the **Makarora / Lake Wanaka** system.
6. Continue east to [Wanaka (NZWF)](/guides/nzwf/) or turn south for [Queenstown (NZQN)](/guides/nzqn/).

## Reporting Points

- **Haast township** - coastal junction.
- **Gates of Haast** - narrow section of the pass road.
- **Haast Pass summit** - saddle.
- **Makarora** - eastern side of the divide.
- **Lake Wanaka** - arrival into the Wanaka basin.

## Minimum Altitudes

- **6,500 ft MSL** over the pass.
- **2,000 ft AGL** minimum over Mount Aspiring National Park.
- **9,500 ft MSL** recommended for Queenstown crossing when weather permits.

## Frequencies

| Service               | Frequency |
| --------------------- | --------- |
| Wanaka CTAF           | 119.1     |
| Queenstown Tower      | 118.1     |
| Area / Mountain radio | 130.6     |

## Hazards

- Narrow valley with limited go-around options.
- Very high annual rainfall - cloud and rain common.
- Mountain wave in strong westerly.
- Terrain on both sides of the pass.

## Tips

- Give yourself plenty of altitude over the pass - you lose it quickly entering the eastern valleys.
- Check the Mount Aspiring National Park overflight rules before departure.

## Overview

The Mount Cook valley runs north from Lake Pukaki into the heart of the Aoraki/Mount Cook National Park. It contains New Zealand's highest mountain and two of its largest glaciers. The airspace is a Mount Cook Flight Information Zone (FIZ) with no ATC.

{% include region_map.html %}

## Route

1. Approach from the south - either from [Omarama (NZOA)](/guides/nzoa/) or [Pukaki (NZUK)](/guides/nzuk/).
2. Fly north along the eastern shore of **Lake Pukaki**.
3. Overfly **Glentanner Station (NZGT)** and the **Tasman River** mouth.
4. Continue into the **Tasman Valley** (east side) or **Hooker Valley** (west side).
5. View the **Tasman Glacier terminal lake** and **Aoraki/Mount Cook** from a safe distance.
6. Return south via the same route.

## Reporting Points

- **Pukaki** - southern entry to the valley.
- **Glentanner** - mid-valley station.
- **Mount Cook Airport (NZMC)** - valley base.
- **Tasman Terminal** - glacier lake viewpoint.
- **Hooker Valley** - Aoraki viewpoint.

## Minimum Altitudes

- **2,000 ft AGL** minimum over national park land.
- **Mount Cook FIZ (Alps Traffic):** 118.6 within 10 NM.
- **One-way valley traffic** - keep to the right-hand side of the valley: eastern side northbound, western side southbound.

## Frequencies

| Service                       | Frequency |
| ----------------------------- | --------- |
| Mount Cook FIZ (Alps Traffic) | 118.6     |
| Area / Mountain radio         | 130.6     |
| Christchurch Information      | 123.5     |

## Hazards

- **High density altitude** - Mount Cook Airport is 2,153 ft AMSL, and density altitude in summer reaches 4,000 ft+.
- **Katabatic winds** off the Tasman and Hooker glaciers.
- **Mountain wave** - the Mount Cook area is notorious.
- High scenic traffic - helicopters, fixed-wing, and gliders.
- The valley is a trap - the only way out is the way you came in.

## Tips

- Reduce payload for high-altitude operations.
- Check the Mount Cook FIZ frequency before entering.
- Do not descend below 2,000 ft AGL over the park.
- Never fly into cloud in the valley - turn back.

- [Pukaki (NZUK)](/guides/nzuk/)

## Overview

Christchurch International is the primary CPAS base and the busiest airport on the South Island. It sits on the Canterbury Plains, bounded to the north-east by the Waimakariri River and to the south by the Port Hills. Expect Class C controlled airspace, heavy airline traffic, and frequent nor'wester wind shifts.

{% include region_map.html %}

## Runways

| ---------- | ------- | ------- | ---------------------------------- |
| 02/20      | 3,288 m | Asphalt | Main jet runway. ILS on both ends. |
| 11/29      | 1,703 m | Asphalt | Crosswind runway, used by GA.      |

## Frequencies

| Service                  | Frequency | Notes                             |
| ------------------------ | --------- | --------------------------------- |
| ATIS                     | 127.2     |                                   |
| Delivery                 | 128.2     | IFR clearances                    |
| Ground                   | 121.9     |                                   |
| Tower                    | 118.4     |                                   |
| Approach                 | 120.9     | Christchurch Approach / Departure |
| Christchurch Information | 129.3     | FIS for the wider area            |

## Elevation

123 ft AMSL.

## Local Procedures

- **GA parking:** Canterbury Aero Club (CAC) apron is the main GA hub.
- **VFR departures:** Expect a clearance to track via the Waimakariri River or the coast.
- **Noise abatement:** Prefer Runway 02 for easterly operations when able.
- **Port Hills transition:** Use CTAF 123.1 in the Port Hills area but remain inside the CTR if cleared.

## Hazards

- Strong nor'wester crosswind on 11/29.
- Airline wake turbulence on 02/20.
- Downwind turbulence over the Port Hills in a nor'wester.
- Frequent low-level agricultural aircraft to the south-west.

## Recommended Approaches

- **Runway 02 (ILS):** From the south-west over the suburbs - expect vectoring.
- **Runway 20 (ILS):** Straight-in from the north-east over Pegasus Bay, minimal terrain.
- **Runway 11/29:** VFR circuit joins, watch for helicopter traffic.
